Behavior guide

Before you scold,
start with “why?”

There is a reason behind the behavior. Understanding what triggers repeated chewing helps you decide what to change at home.

Start by observing

The same chewing,
with different reasons.

These are clues to observe, not a diagnosis of the cause.

🔎

New things look tasty?

Puppies explore with their mouths and may chew more during teething. Offer chew toys suited to their age and size.

⚡

A busy mouth when bored

Dogs may look for something to chew when bored or short on play. Make walks, play and exploration part of the daily routine.

🏠

Worse when home alone?

Record barking, pacing and other signs too. If you suspect separation-related anxiety, consult a professional instead of relying on the product alone.

Three things to try today

Beyond the furniture,
give them a better choice.

01 · Manage the space

Keep tempting objects out of reach

Tidy cords and small objects, and limit access to frequently chewed areas.

02 · Offer an alternative

Redirect attention to a toy

Praise your dog for chewing an appropriate toy. Avoid scolding after the behavior has already happened.

03 · Keep a record

Note when and what they chew

Record the time, object, whether they were alone and the preceding activity to help identify useful changes.

Some situations need extra help.

Prevent access if there is a risk of swallowing or injury. For persistent behavior or severe anxiety, consult a vet or behavior professional. The personality check helps organize observations.
